# The verdict: GUILTY, 12 to 0

**The case:** Case: A student team wants to build "Clara", an AI tool that helps neurodivergent (autistic and ADHD) university students turn confusing assignment instructions, big tasks, and deadlines into an actionable personal plan. The plan is to ship it within one week as a fully functional, production-deployed MVP with validated test cases, pitch it at the North American AI Challenge with the partner "Surfeando la Neurodiversidad", and grow it into a sustainable business.

**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## Charges that stuck

1. **Too big, with nobody to explain it.** Thirteen features in a week, no stack and no named coder risks the rule that a human must "review, explain, and validate all submitted code" (6 jurors).
2. **Not shown to beat free tools.** Students already use ChatGPT as a "translator," nothing compares Clara with it, and the planner looks like "another focus app" (5 jurors).
3. **"Validated" is untrue.** No partner interviews yet; the kill test needs briefs the team only "hopes to get" (4 jurors; 4 more said this of revenue).

## What the defense got right

1. **The decoder is a real idea.** Quoting the brief and listing ambiguities answers the call for "a distinct, research-informed AI capability." Two jurors praised it.
2. **A small version is buildable.** One host, database and LLM, plus the "Clara Lite" fallback, is defensible.

## Conditions for acquittal

1. **Commit only to Clara Lite or the P0 core** (7 jurors).
2. **Name the coders, pick the stack, and have them review every AI-written commit** (5 jurors; most need 1 and 2 together).
3. **Drop "validated"; ask the partner in writing now for real briefs and a Clara-versus-ChatGPT test with Neurosurfers** (4 jurors).

## The sentence

The idea survives; the case as charged does not. Restate it as Clara Lite, owned by people you name today. Write a consent plan before any Neurosurfer touches it. No payer can sign before Nov 5, so pitch the business as a hypothesis.
